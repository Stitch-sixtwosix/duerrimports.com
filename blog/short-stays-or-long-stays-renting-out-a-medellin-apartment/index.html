Short stays or long stays: renting out a Medellín apartment

Nightly guests or monthly ones? How the two models differ for apartment owners in Medellín, and what to check before you choose.

November 20, 2026 · Duerr Imports · 3 min read

If you own a furnished apartment in Medellín, you have two broad ways to rent it out: by the night to visitors, or by the month to people staying longer. Both can work. They differ in the rules that apply, the work involved and how steady the income is.

This post explains the two models and gives a short list of things to settle before you pick one. The company details come from the website of Medellín Concierge, which manages both kinds of rental in the city.

What counts as a long stay

Medellín Concierge defines a long stay as any booking of 30 nights or more. Anything shorter is a short stay, the kind most people associate with Airbnb.

A long stay in this sense is still a furnished rental booked through a platform. It is not the same as a traditional residential lease, where an agency finds one tenant, often for an unfurnished apartment at a fixed monthly rent.

Start with the building, not the listing

The first question is whether you are allowed to host by the night at all.

According to Medellín Concierge, two things have to be in place before an apartment takes short stays:

  • The building's rules. The reglamento de propiedad horizontal, the rules of the building, must allow short stays. Many residential buildings restrict them.
  • A tourism registration. The property needs an active Registro Nacional de Turismo (RNT), which the company says is renewed every year.

If your building does not allow short stays, monthly stays are usually the better fit. Whether an apartment rented only for 30 nights or more needs an RNT depends on how it is rented, so check your own case.

Rules change. Medellín Concierge states these points as of November 2026 and notes that it manages registrations but does not give legal or tax advice. Rental income in Colombia can also carry tax obligations, especially for owners who live abroad. Confirm yours with a qualified professional.

How the two models compare

Work. A short-stay apartment has a check-in, a check-out and a cleaning after every booking, plus guest messages at all hours. A long-stay apartment may have only a few turnovers a year.

Income. Short stays can earn more in peak season, but the income varies from month to month. Long stays give up some of those peak nights in exchange for a steadier, more predictable result.

Wear. Fewer turnovers mean lower cleaning costs and less wear on the apartment.

Guests. Short stays draw tourists and people on brief business trips. Monthly stays draw remote workers, professionals on assignment and families getting to know the city.

Management cost. Because short stays take more handling, managers usually charge a higher percentage for them. Medellín Concierge's site lists its Basic plan at 12% of rental income for long stays and 17% for short stays, and its Premium plan at 15% and 20%. IVA, the Colombian value added tax, is added to the commission.

Which apartments suit which model

Long stays tend to suit apartments on residential streets, buildings that limit short stays, and owners who prefer a calmer, steadier income. Short stays suit apartments in buildings that allow them, in areas visitors want to be in, with an owner or manager ready to handle frequent turnovers.

Questions to settle before you decide

  1. Does the building's reglamento allow stays of fewer than 30 nights? Read it, or ask the building administration.
  2. Is the apartment furnished, or are you willing to furnish it?
  3. Who will answer guests, handle check-ins and arrange cleaning, especially if you live outside Colombia?
  4. Can you accept income that changes from month to month, or do you need it to be steady?
  5. If you use a manager, what does the commission cover, when are you paid, and how much can they spend on repairs without asking you? Get the answers in writing.
  6. Which registrations and taxes apply to you? Ask a qualified professional.

Where to go from here

If you are not sure which model fits your apartment, Medellín Concierge offers a free consultation and has an income calculator on its site for a rough estimate. Both are at medellinconcierge.net.
