Eminent domain cases start years before the notice

A taking moves through public stages long before an owner hears about it. Here is what each stage means for an eminent domain practice.

November 18, 2026 · Duerr Imports · 3 min read

Most property owners learn about a taking when an offer letter arrives. By then the project has usually been public for years. It has been on a planning list, through environmental review and onto a right-of-way plan. For an eminent domain attorney, that gap is where the work of finding clients really happens.

This post walks through the stages a public project passes through before land is acquired, and what each one tells a practice.

1. The planning list

Roads, bridges, pipelines and other public works usually begin as entries on a plan: a state transportation improvement program, a regional long-range plan, a utility's capital plan. An entry may name a corridor, a rough location and a budget year.

At this stage nothing is certain. Projects are delayed, redesigned or dropped. But a project on a funded list is the earliest public sign that land along a route may be needed.

2. Environmental review

Larger projects, especially those with federal money, go through environmental review. The documents produced here often include maps of alternative routes and the land each one would affect.

This is often the first time a project's footprint can be matched to specific parcels. When the alternatives narrow to a preferred route, the list of affected owners becomes much clearer.

3. The right-of-way plan

Once a design is chosen, the agency prepares right-of-way plans showing exactly which land, or which part of a parcel, it needs. Partial takings, temporary construction easements and access changes all show up here.

For a practice, this is where the questions that drive a case begin: how much of the parcel is taken, what happens to the remainder, and whether access or use is affected.

4. The offer

The acquiring agency appraises the property and makes an offer. For many owners this is the first contact, and they may feel pressure to accept quickly. The offer is also where an owner's need for advice is most immediate.

5. The declaration of taking

If the owner and the agency do not agree, the agency can file to condemn. From here the case is in court, and the question is mainly what compensation is just.

What this means for a practice

Each stage is public, but the records are scattered across agencies, formats and websites. Following them by hand for a whole state is slow, and the owners who will be affected are rarely named in the plans themselves.
