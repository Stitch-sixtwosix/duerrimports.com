If you have asked a "we buy houses" company for an offer, the number probably came in well below what similar homes nearby have sold for. That gap is not arbitrary. A cash buyer works backward from what the house will be worth after it is fixed up, and each step can be written down.

This post walks through that math, explains when a cash sale makes sense, and lists what to ask any buyer before you sign. The company details come from the website of [FastFlip Realty](https://fastflip.co/), which buys houses directly from owners.

## Start with the value after repairs

The first number is not what your house is worth today. It is what the house should sell for once it has been renovated. Investors call this the after-repair value.

A careful buyer bases it on recent sales of comparable homes nearby that are already in good condition.

## The investor price

An investor does not pay the full after-repair value. They take a percentage of it, to cover the cost of holding the house during the work, the cost of selling it afterward, the risk that something goes wrong, and their profit.

A common rule of thumb in the business is 70 percent. FastFlip's site uses that figure in its pricing example and labels it the investor price.

## Then repairs and the buyer's fee

From the investor price, the buyer subtracts the estimated cost of repairs. If the company is going to pass the house to another investor, or charges for its own work, its fee comes off as well.

FastFlip's site shows the whole calculation with an example:

1. Value after repairs: $180,000
2. Investor price at 70 percent: $126,000
3. Less repairs: $35,000
4. Less the company's fee: $10,000
5. Cash offer: $81,000

These are the site's illustration, not a quote for any real house. A written offer follows real sold homes and the condition of the house. If a buyer cannot show you the lines behind their number, ask why.

## What you get for the lower price

A cash sale trades price for speed and certainty. According to FastFlip's site, the company buys houses as is, charges no commission or fees, pays the title company's closing costs, sends a written offer within 48 hours and closes in 7 to 30 days on a date the seller picks. There is one walkthrough instead of a series of showings, and no bank approval to wait on.

A listing works the other way. The site's own comparison says a listing often gets a higher price, with a commission that is often 5 to 6 percent, repairs and cleaning that usually fall on the seller, and a closing that often takes 60 to 90 days. It describes those as typical ranges, not guarantees.

## When a listing is the better choice

FastFlip's site is direct about this: if your house is in good shape and you can wait a few months, listing will likely net you more.

A cash offer is for the other cases. The house needs major repairs you cannot pay for. It is full of belongings, has tenants, or has damage. Or you need a closing date you can count on because of a move, an estate or a debt.

## What to ask any cash buyer

- **Which sold homes did you use?** The after-repair value should come from real nearby sales.
- **What is your repair estimate, and what is in it?** A padded estimate lowers your offer.
- **What is your fee, and where will I see it?** FastFlip says its fee appears on the settlement statement.
- **Who holds the money?** Deposits and sale funds should be held by an independent title company or closing attorney, not by the buyer.
- **Will you assign the contract?** Some buyers pass the contract to another investor. FastFlip says it sometimes does, that the agreement says so up front, and that the seller's price and date do not change.
- **Do you represent me?** A direct buyer is on the other side of the deal. FastFlip states that it is not a real estate agent or broker and buys as a principal.

Any mortgage or liens are normally paid off from the sale at closing, so ask for an estimate of what you will actually receive. A sale can also have tax consequences. Have an attorney read the agreement before you sign, and ask a tax professional about your own situation.
